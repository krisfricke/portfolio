From the moment

I got in the registration line,

it was clear they’re on a

different page concerning varroa than us down here in Vic — “how’s your

varroa levels?” “18 in my highest hives” “oh that’s a bit much this time

of year,” all around me they were casually discussing their latest varroa

counts and treatment plans!

Another frequent subject of immediate conversation was getting bogged

trying to access bee sites and the marvel that it actually wasn’t raining,

apparently it has recently been wet in NSW.

The presentations as one might expect strongly featured varroa mite

treatments, but also touched on other issues, and then as an added scare

Tropilaelaps mites showed up in two presentations.

On the varroa front, Miguel Rodriguez, managing

director of Veto-

Pharma (France based manufacturer of Bayvarol and Apivar) presented

about their company. Jon Lockwood apparently recently visited their

facility in

France. Jon Lockwood also presented about what his company

(Goldfields Honey) is doing for mite management. Most controversially

he floated the idea that using Apivar with honey supers may not be a

problem, citing a study’ which says amitraz degrades very quicky within

colonies, 10 days in honey and

71 day in beeswax, and that even at 10

times the normal dosage, amitraz residues were not found in the honey or

wax when analyzed 28 days after the end of treatment.
