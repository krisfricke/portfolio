This is a very interesting issue and I’d like to do an in-depth analysis of

this paper and what is known about DMPF and DMA, but AgVic won't let

Anyway, Dr Fauvel’s presentation was very interesting. Nearly no one is

probably better able to speak to what is done as a whole by commercial

beekeepers in the States than she, as the BIP teams she coordinated for

years met with commercial beekeepers to help them monitor and deal with

their varroa issues. I’d say they were like long-term VDOs but they seemed

to do a lot more freeze-kill assays then VDOs are doing. Beekeepers

who engaged with the BIP teams apparently consistently had 13% less

losses. She talked about trends over time in miticide use in the States as

popular miticides became ineffective due to resistance (the pyrethroids

such as Bayvarol), or were found to be overly toxic (coumaphos). Another

interesting highlight she mentioned was that commercial beekeepers in

the states really don’t find oxalic fogging a practical method of treatment.

It seems to have gained a great enthusiasm here, which I feel like can

perhaps being attributed to it having an outsized representation on that

popular learning source known as Youtube, being as it makes for more

interesting videos than simply putting boring old strips into hives. On

the second day Dr Fauvel spoke again, this time about putting hives in

refrigerated warehouses. In addition to her informative overview she linked

us to a good video about it and a thorough pdf guide.? She emphasized

not underestimating the power requirements for sucha

facility.
