After a day off, which some from out of town used to explore the beautiful

area, though some others complained unnecessarily lengthened their trip

to the area, the VAA conference began on Thursday. It began very strong

out the gate witha talk titled “Optimizing the Effectiveness of Oxalic Acid”

by Dr Cameron Jack followed by a presentation via zoom by Randy

Oliver. My main takeaway from Dr Jack's presentation was that while OA

has its positives,

it really struggles to even prevent mite numbers from

increasing during Spring and peak times. He highlighted that “fogging”

and “vaporization” refer to different forms of application and noted that

fogging is particularly not very effective. Randy emphasized constant mite

monitoring and the ease of selecting for resistance merely by breeding off

your best queens — in his case beginning with just a handful of queens

from 1000 hives. He also mentioned the need to maintain genetic diversity

and run a minimum of 30 breeding lines. These summations of course

are only pulling out key points

I recall without the context around them,

I highly recommend attending annual conferences to get the complete

explanations direct from the world experts such as these.
