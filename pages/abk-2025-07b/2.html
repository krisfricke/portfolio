“Two years ago the Crop Pollination Association of Australia was down

to 16 members,” CPAA President Steve Fuller addressed the room

of about 80 gathered for this year's meeting, highlighting the ups and

downs the association has had. Every year the CPAA has their AGM in

association with a different state convention, in this case the Victorian

Apiarists Association will meet in the same venue later in the week.

Also highlighting the current momentum of the association, while many

beekeeping groups at all levels struggle to find enough willing volunteers

to hold offices, they had nine very qualified candidates vying for election

to the seven executive seats.

The CPAA

differs from the various state and national beekeeping

associations in that, of course, it represents the pollination industry very

specifically.

Pollination very often brings beekeepers across interstate

borders, necessitating such an interstate group as the CPAA.
